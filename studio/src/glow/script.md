# You are glowing right now (Mind Matter, general theme)

Approved by the owner on 17 November 2026. 317 words, about 2 minutes. Narrator: Brian.

Right now, your body is giving off light.
Not heat. Real, visible light.
It is just a thousand times too faint for your eyes to see.
In 2009, scientists in Japan set out to photograph it.
They sealed a room so that not a single ray of outside light could get in.
Five young men sat in front of a camera in total darkness.
The camera was so sensitive it could pick up single particles of light.
Each picture took twenty minutes.
And in those pictures, the men were glowing.
The light was strongest on the face: the cheeks, the forehead and the neck.
And it changed through the day.
It was dimmest at ten in the morning and brightest around four in the afternoon, in step with the body's internal clock.
So where does it come from?
Every cell in your body burns fuel to stay alive.
That burning makes highly reactive molecules called free radicals.
When they crash into the fats and proteins in your cells, they pass on a little energy.
Those molecules hold the energy for a moment, then let it go as a tiny flash of light.
It is the same kind of chemistry that makes a glow stick shine.
In 2025, scientists in Canada took the idea further.
They filmed living mice in the dark, and the mice glowed.
After the mice died, the glow almost vanished.
Plants showed something similar.
Where a leaf was damaged, it glowed brighter, because its stressed cells were making more of those reactive molecules.
Scientists hope this light could one day help doctors check the health of tissue without touching it, or help farmers spot sick crops early.
This is not a mysterious aura.
It is chemistry, the side effect of your cells at work.
So the next time you sit in a dark room, remember.
You are still glowing.
You just can't see it.

## Sources checked

- Salari, Oblak et al. (2025), J. Phys. Chem. Lett. — living vs dead mice; damaged leaves glow more; reactive oxygen species; glow-stick comparison; not an aura.
