# Undercover: The Soldiers Who Lifted a Tank (script, 20 November 2026)

**Status:** draft, facts checked; waiting for the owner's go-ahead before voicing.
**Structure (owner's request, 20 Nov 2026):** mystery. Open on someone doing something that makes no sense, add stranger clues, and keep the "why" hidden until the last 15 s.
**Target length:** about 58 s (about 140 words).

## Script

Gold key word per line in **bold**.

1. France, 1944. Four soldiers lift a forty-ton **tank**.
2. Two Frenchmen on bicycles **freeze**.
3. "How?" one asks. A soldier shrugs. "The Americans are very **strong**."
4. That night, it gets **stranger**.
5. A bulldozer drives in circles around an **empty** field.
6. Trucks with giant speakers blast tank **noises**. All night long.
7. Next morning, an artist paints badges for a division that isn't **there**.
8. In town, a man in a general's uniform shows off loudly. He isn't a **general**.
9. Overhead, a German plane takes **photos** of it all.
10. Because there are no real tanks here. They're **rubber**.
11. Blown up from a bag in twenty **minutes**.
12. Eleven hundred men, pretending to be a whole **division**.
13. So the Germans look here, while the real attack comes **somewhere** else.
14. They became known as the Ghost **Army**.
15. Their secret stayed hidden until **1996**.
16. Back in the field, a soldier pulls a **plug**.

## How the mystery is paced

- 0-3 s: the impossible picture (men lifting a tank). Question planted: how?
- 3-8 s: the soldier dodges the question with a joke. Mystery stays open.
- 8-30 s: four clues, each weirder: tracks into an empty field, tank sounds with no tanks, badges for a missing division, a fake general. The viewer starts guessing.
- 30-35 s: the German plane: the first hint of why.
- 35-50 s: reveal: rubber tanks, a fake army, so the real attack lands elsewhere.
- Last 2 s: the plug is pulled. Hiss. Cut. Loops back to the tank in frame 0.

## Fact check

- Ghost Army = 23rd Headquarters Special Troops, about 1,100 men, formed 1944; inflatable tanks, sound trucks, fake radio, phony generals; impersonated far larger divisions: Wikipedia "Ghost Army"; National WWII Museum; History.com; Smithsonian.
- Two Frenchmen on bicycles saw four GIs lift a "40-ton Sherman tank"; Arthur Shilstone told them "The Americans are very strong": Smithsonian (two articles); Slate/Beyer & Sayles; Euronews interview; ghostarmy.com.
- Bulldozers made fake tank tracks for aerial reconnaissance: 99% Invisible; rarehistoricalphotos (citing Beyer).
- Speakers on half-tracks played recorded sounds of tanks and troops, heard up to 15 miles away, night after night: Wikipedia; National WWII Museum; National Gallery of Art.
- Artists painted fake unit markings on bumpers; officers impersonated generals: National WWII Museum; McGraw Hill (mheducation).
- Inflatable tank about 93 lb, unrolled from a bag and inflated in about 20 minutes: ghostarmy.com; Atlas Obscura.
- Secret until declassified in 1996; "Ghost Army" nickname came after the war: Wikipedia; National WWII Museum; 99% Invisible.
- Invented for illustration: "How?" (the sources only describe the Frenchmen as baffled), the glasses, the café scene staging, and the soldier pulling the plug.

## Description

France, 1944. Two Frenchmen on bicycles watched four American soldiers lift a 40-ton Sherman tank. "The Americans are very strong," one soldier told them. The truth was stranger: the 23rd Headquarters Special Troops, about 1,100 artists, sound engineers and soldiers, used rubber tanks, giant speakers and fake radio traffic to pretend to be whole divisions, pulling German attention away from the real attacks. They became known as the Ghost Army, and their story stayed secret until 1996.

The staging is a dramatised illustration; the Ghost Army and the "very strong" story are real.
