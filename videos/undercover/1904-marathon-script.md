# Undercover: The Worst Olympic Marathon Ever Run (script, 20 November 2026)

**Target length:** about 58 s (140 words at Albert's ~143 wpm).

## What's new in this one (aimed at better retention)

- **Visual hook in frame 0:** the strange picture is on screen and moving before the first word ends.
- **Open loop at 4 s:** "And that's not the weirdest part" promises more and carries viewers through the middle.
- **One continuous camera move:** the whole video is one smooth glide down one road. No scene rebuilding; very satisfying to watch.
- **Built-in progress bar:** mile posts tick past (MILE 0 ... 9 ... 18 ... 24), so viewers feel the race moving toward a finish.
- **A beat every 3-4 s,** each funnier than the last; the ending loops straight back into frame 0.

## Script (140 words)

Gold key word per line in **bold**.

1. He finished the Olympic marathon first. He rode eleven miles of it in a **car**.
2. And that's not the **weirdest** part.
3. St. Louis, **1904**. A boiling hot day.
4. The organizer allows almost no **water**. "Thirst makes you tough," he says.
5. Mile nine. One runner quits and hops in a **car**.
6. A Cuban mailman eats apples off a tree. They're **rotten**. So he takes a nap.
7. He still finishes **fourth**.
8. And the leader? His own team feeds him rat **poison**.
9. Mixed into egg whites. With **brandy**.
10. Then the car **breaks** down. Car guy jogs the rest.
11. The crowd cheers. The President's daughter is about to give him **gold**.
12. Then he admits it. "It was a **joke**."
13. The real **winner** staggers in, held up by his trainers.
14. The **slowest** Olympic marathon ever.
15. And the man in the **car**?
16. The next year, he won the Boston Marathon. On **foot**.

## Shot plan: one road, one camera glide

The whole video is a single forward tracking shot along a dusty country road at runner height (camera can rise or swing, never hard-cut). Wooden mile posts pass in rhythm with the music. Each beat is a set piece the camera glides up to.

| Line | Picture |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Frame 0 (visual hook):** a sweaty runner trudges toward camera. A putt-putting 1904 open car overtakes him; in the passenger seat a runner wearing race number 31, feet up on the door, waves. Dust puff. All moving from the first frame. |
| 2 | Car pulls ahead; camera follows, picking up speed. |

| 10 | MILE 19. Car hits a bump; a wheel pops off and rolls past camera (satisfying). #31 climbs out and jogs. |

## Fact check

- 1904 St. Louis Olympic marathon, 30 August, 32 starters, about 90°F: Wikipedia "Athletics at the 1904 Summer Olympics – Men's marathon"; Olympedia; Outside.
- Fred Lorz dropped out around mile 9, rode about 11 miles in a car, which broke down around mile 19; he jogged to the stadium, was hailed as winner and was about to receive the gold from Alice Roosevelt; said it was a joke; banned (later lifted); won the 1905 Boston Marathon: Wikipedia; Smithsonian; Olympics.com; AOL/History.
- Organizer James Sullivan limited water on purpose to study "purposeful dehydration" (one or two water points, sources differ, so "almost no water"): Smithsonian; Olympics.com; Popular Science; Wikipedia.
- Félix Carvajal, Cuban mailman, ate rotten apples from an orchard, got cramps, napped, finished fourth: Olympics.com; Wikipedia; Popular Science.
- Thomas Hicks given small doses of strychnine (rat poison, a stimulant in tiny doses) with egg whites and brandy by his trainers; near collapse, hallucinating, supported to the finish; 3:28:53 is the slowest Olympic marathon time: Wikipedia; Smithsonian; Olympics.com; Outside.
- Invented for illustration: the organizer's spoken line, the bowler hat and bucket, the exact staging of each shot.

## Description

The 1904 Olympic marathon in St. Louis might be the strangest race ever run. The organizer kept water to a minimum on purpose. One runner napped after eating rotten apples, and still came fourth. The leader was kept going by his own trainers with rat poison, egg whites and brandy. And the first man across the line had ridden 11 miles of it in a car.

The organizer's line and the staging are a dramatised illustration; the race is real.
