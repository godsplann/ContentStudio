# Undercover draft script: Clever Hans (18 November 2026)

Status: draft, facts checked, waiting for the owner's OK and a narrator. About 165 words, about 60 s.
This is a documented event with real names, so the facts stay as the sources give them. The final "tapping and tapping" is a dramatised picture of Hans failing when the questioner doesn't know the answer.

## Script

A horse just solved a calendar puzzle by tapping his hoof eleven times.
You see, it's 1904 in Berlin. A retired maths teacher, Wilhelm von Osten, has asked his horse Hans: if the eighth of the month is a Tuesday, what's the date of the following Friday?
Eleven taps. Correct.
Hans adds, subtracts, even tells the time. Von Osten never charges a penny to watch. The New York Times writes about him.
So a commission of thirteen experts examines the horse, and finds no trick at all.
Then a young psychologist, Oskar Pfungst, tries something simple. He puts blinkers on Hans so he can't see who's asking.
And the genius horse gets almost everything wrong.
So what was going on?
Well, it's called the Clever Hans effect. As the taps got close to the right answer, whoever was asking would tense up, then relax, without even knowing it.
Hans couldn't count. He was reading faces.
So Pfungst asks a question nobody in the room knows the answer to.
And Hans starts tapping. And tapping. And tapping.
