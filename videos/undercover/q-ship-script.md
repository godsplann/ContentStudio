# Undercover: Q-ships (draft script, 18 November 2026)

**Status:** draft for the owner. Not voiced yet.
**Working title:** Why This Crew Abandoned a Perfectly Good Ship

## Script (about 150 words, about 55 s)

The captain ordered his crew to abandon ship. Nothing was wrong with it.

North Atlantic, 1916. German submarines are hunting lone cargo ships.

Torpedoes are precious, so a sub would rather surface and finish small ships with its deck gun.

And this rusty old steamer looks like the easiest target on the ocean.

Every morning, the captain drills his crew in one thing: running away.

A new deckhand frowns. "Sir, shouldn't we practise fighting?"

"Practise falling into a lifeboat."

Then a periscope breaks the water.

The crew scrambles into the boats, dropping oars, shouting, one man still holding his teacup.

The submarine rises, slow and confident, and creeps toward the empty ship.

Except it isn't empty.

Half the crew is still aboard, lying flat behind the rails, not breathing.

The Navy had a name for these decoys: Q-ships. The men in the boats were the panic party, and every scream was rehearsed.

The sub slides closer. The captain nods.

And the chicken coop on deck falls open.

## Shot notes

- Opening: captain on the bridge pointing at the boats; calm sea, nothing burning.
- Place-and-year beat: "1916" stencilled on a crate as the camera passes.
- Light: grey dawn, low sun through sea haze, the periscope glinting.
- Last frame: the coop's side drops and the barrel of a gun swings out. Cut on that.

## Fact check

- Q-ships were armed merchant ships with hidden guns, built to lure U-boats into attacking on the surface. Sources: Wikipedia "Q-ship"; Historic UK "Britain's WWI Mystery Q-Ships".
- U-boats saved torpedoes and preferred to sink small ships with deck guns. Sources: Wikipedia "Q-ship"; Globe Composite "Q-ships vs U-boats".
- The rehearsed "panic party" that abandoned ship in a show of confusion while gun crews stayed hidden. Sources: Western Front Association "Pantomime at Sea"; History of War (via PressReader); 1919 Evening Post report on HMS Farnborough (Papers Past).
- Guns hidden in hen-coops and behind hinged sides. Source: Historic UK.
- HMS Farnborough (Q-5) sank U-68 on 22 March 1916, matching "1916". Sources: Western Front Association; warhistory.org.
- Invented for illustration (not presented as record): the deckhand, his line, the teacup, the morning drills as worded. The captain is unnamed.
