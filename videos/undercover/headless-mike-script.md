# Undercover: The Chicken That Lived Without a Head (script, 19 November 2026)

**Status:** facts checked; waiting for the owner's go-ahead before voicing.
**Working title:** The Chicken That Lived Without a Head
**Target length:** under 75 s (owner: under a minute, 1:15 at most).

## Script (151 words, about 64 s at Albert's pace)

Opens with a one-line hook (first 2-3 s), then the formula.

This chicken lived a year and a half without a head.

Colorado, 1945. A farmer chops off a rooster's head for dinner.

But the rooster gets up. He tries to peck. He even tries to crow.

Next morning, he's still alive. So the farmer names him Mike.

He feeds Mike with an eyedropper, straight down his throat.

A neighbour laughs. "Lloyd, a chicken with no head is called dinner."

So the farmer bets him a beer. And wins.

Soon people pay a quarter to see Mike.

Scientists check him over. The axe missed the brain stem.

That little part runs breathing and the heartbeat.

So Mike lives on. Month after month.

There's one danger. Sometimes Mike chokes.

The farmer always carries a tiny syringe to clear his throat.

One night, in a motel, Mike starts choking.

The farmer grabs his bag. The syringe isn't there.

He left it at the show.

## Shot notes

- Reveal glow: a soft glow on a simple brain-stem diagram the scientist holds up.
- Last frame: the farmer's hands in the open bag, the empty slot. Cut. Keep it gentle: no shot of Mike dying.

## Fact check

- 10 September 1945, Lloyd Olsen of Fruita, Colorado, beheaded the bird for dinner; it kept trying to peck and crow; found alive next morning. Sources: Scientific American blog; Guinness World Records; TIME.
- Fed by eyedropper into the oesophagus. Sources: Guinness; Britannica; Scientific American.
- Olsen bet neighbours beer that he had a live headless chicken. Sources: BBC via The Ghost in My Machine; Medium (OMGFacts).
- Sideshow tour at 25 cents a look. Sources: Ripley's; miketheheadlesschicken.org.
- University scientists found most of the brain stem left intact (sources disagree on the jugular, so it isn't mentioned). Sources: National Geographic; Snopes; Britannica.
- Lived about 18 months. Sources: Guinness; TIME; Britannica.
- Died choking in a Phoenix motel, March 1947; the syringe had been left at the sideshow. Sources: Britannica; Snopes (BBC); Atlas Obscura.
- Invented for illustration: the neighbour's line.
