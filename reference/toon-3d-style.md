# Toon-3D story style: reference notes

What three sample videos from another channel do (the videos were deleted after analysis), and how Mind Matter builds the same look in `studio/`.

- **Analysed:** 16 November 2026, from every frame for the measurements and from one-frame-per-second contact sheets plus six-frames-per-second strips for the visual notes.
- **Samples:** "How Mine Salting Worked" (mine), "How One Cart Stopped an Entire Army" (cart), "The Instinct That Makes You Crash" (crash). They are another channel's videos.
- **Not analysed:** the sound. Only its loudness and gaps were measured; nobody listened to the voice, music or effects.
- **Rule:** learn the technique, never the content. Do not copy the samples' characters, stories, jokes, signs or colour choices.

## 1. The style in one paragraph

Flat, inked 2D cartoon characters act inside a fully lit, textured 3D world. The camera never rests: it pushes in, glides, cranes and whips from one place to the next. Light is strong and directional, with long shadows, glowing lamps, haze and soft background blur. Captions show one or two words at a time, in step with the voice. There is no logo, header or caption box on screen: the picture fills the whole frame.

What the numbers mean:

- **The camera is always moving.** Not one frame in three minutes has a locked-off camera.
- **Cuts are optional.** The cart video has no hard cut in 67 seconds: it changes place with fast camera moves and changes time of day inside the shot. The other two cut every three to four seconds.
- **Captions run word by word**, at least 160 words a minute.
- **The sound never stops.** There is no pause in the voice or music longer than 0.4 seconds.
- **Brightness swings widely** inside one video (night and storm scenes against bright daylight), but the average stays around the middle.

## 3. Characters

- Flat 2D drawings, not 3D models. They always face the camera.
- A large round head (about a quarter to a third of the height), a small bean-shaped body, very thin stick arms and legs, and a thick dark outline of even width.
- Heads are plain and light; identity comes from one or two features: a hat, a moustache, glasses, a uniform, a coloured coat.
- The face does the acting with very few parts: brows, dot eyes or ring eyes, a small mouth. Expressions swap whole (calm, smug, shocked, furious, grinning), they do not morph slowly.
- Characters are not fully unlit. They take a tint from the scene: dimmed and blue in night rain, orange when a glowing sign lights the face, a soft shade on one side.
- They cast a real shadow in the shape of their silhouette on the ground and on walls. This is the main thing that makes a flat drawing sit in the 3D world.
- Three to six characters per video. Crowds are the same drawing repeated many times with small changes.

## 4. The world

- Fully modelled 3D places with painted textures: stone blocks, wooden planks, cracked rock, tarmac with road markings, grass made of many separate blades.
- Dense with detail. Every view holds many objects: lanterns, barrels, crates, fences, banners, signs, tools, loose stones.
- Props are richer than the characters. Hero props (a gold pan, a rocket cart, a patrol car, a marquee arrow sign) have highlights, glass, metal and readable lettering.
- Signs carry information and jokes: a counter board, a speed display, a shop sign, a newspaper front page. A counter that changes is how the video shows time passing.
- About five to eight different places per minute, and the same place often returns in different light (day, dusk, night rain, sunrise).

## 5. Lighting and shadows

- **Daylight:** one warm, low sun as the key light and a blue sky as the fill. Shadows are long, soft-edged and slightly blue. Trees throw dappled shade on the road.
- **Interiors:** dark rooms with pools of warm light from lamps you can see in the shot (lanterns, candles, a ceiling lamp). Light falls off quickly into brown shadow. A lamp can throw a visible cone of light with dust specks floating in it.
- **Night and storm:** cool blue ambient light, with small warm lamps as the contrast. Rain is drawn as thin streaks. Lamps and windows glow.
- **Sunrise and dusk:** pink and orange sky, strong rim light, very long shadows, a soft glow around the sun.
- **Glow:** anything that emits light blooms: sign bulbs, lantern glass, a burning fuse, headlights, angry glowing eyes.
- **Depth:** far things are hazier and paler. In close-ups the background is softly out of focus.
- **Light as emotion:** when a character turns furious, the sky darkens and lightning strikes in the same shot. The light follows the story, not the clock.
- **Contact shadows:** everything touches the ground with a shadow. Nothing floats.

## 6. Camera

- **Lens:** wide angle and close to the subject, which exaggerates depth. Often low to the ground with grass or rails in the foreground.
- **Slow push-in:** the default on almost every held shot.
- **Glide between nearby places:** the camera flies around a wall or down a track instead of cutting.
- **Whip:** a very fast, blurred swing to jump to a far place. Used a few times per video, not on every change.
- **Crane and aerial:** rising to a top-down view of a road or a field to show scale, then dropping back.
- **Macro inserts:** roughly a fifth to a third of the running time (judged from the contact sheets) has no character as the subject. The camera sits on a prop instead: gold flakes on a knife, a drilled board, a fuse, a bank note.
- **Follow shots:** the camera travels with a moving thing: a nugget flying into a wall, a spark racing along a rope, a car on a road.
- **Point of view:** through a windscreen with the wheel and dashboard in the foreground, or along a gun barrel.
- **Shot sizes vary constantly:** wide, medium, close-up, macro, aerial. Two neighbouring shots are rarely the same size.
- **Impact shake:** a short jolt on crashes and hits.

## 7. Transitions

- Hard cut (most common in two of the three samples).
- Camera glide with no cut.
- Whip with motion blur.
- Zoom through an object: the camera pushes into a character's pupil and comes out inside a car at night.
- Time-lapse inside a shot: the sky changes from day to dusk to night while the camera keeps moving.

## 8. Animation and acting

- Characters move like paper puppets: the body leans, bends and bounces; arms and legs are simple lines that swing.
- Poses and expressions change in a few frames, on or just before the spoken word they belong to.
- Every sentence is shown literally. If the script says a hand slaps the ground, the ground shatters.
- Physics is exaggerated for comedy: a car flips end over end, a tree drops on a truck, the earth cracks under an angry stamp.
- 3D props move with real weight (wheels turn, doors open, rope coils), while characters stay flat. The contrast is part of the look.
- Something small always moves: dust, leaves, smoke, rain, sparks, flags.

## 9. Effects

Dust puffs on impacts, smoke plumes, sparks, rain streaks, lightning, debris chunks, flying bank notes, light shafts, bloom on anything that glows. Each video has one large signature effect tied to its story (a wall of rockets, a shattered road, a gold vein).

## 10. Captions

- One or two words at a time, each on screen about as long as it is spoken.
- Heavy rounded sans-serif, white with a solid black outline. No box, no shadow, no colour highlights.
- Centred, with the caption's middle about 70 % of the way down the frame.
- Each caption pops in over a few frames.
- Sentence case with the script's own punctuation.

## 11. Story shape

- Frame 0 is already the action. There is no title card, intro or logo.
- The first sentence states a strange result (a man fires gold into a mine wall; cars steer into the only tree on an empty road). The rest of the video explains why.
- One idea per sentence and one picture per sentence.
- The last seconds return to the opening image or land a twist.
- About 60 seconds, at 160 words a minute or a little more.

## 12. How Mind Matter builds it (tested in "What is a GPU?")

The code is in `studio/src/gpu/`. Reuse these pieces; build new worlds, characters and shots for each topic.

| What the samples do | What the GPU video does | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Flat characters in a 3D world | Characters drawn in code on a canvas (head, hat, face, pose), put on a plane that turns to face the camera and leans back when the camera is high | `toon.ts`, `flat.tsx` |
| Silhouette shadows | `castShadow` plus a `customDepthMaterial` with the same texture and `alphaTest` 0.5 | `flat.tsx` |
| Scene tint on characters | The character material's colour set per world (warm white at the desk, cool white inside the computer) | `Flat` `tint` |
| Crowds | One `InstancedMesh` per pose; about 1,100 characters rendered without trouble | `makeCrowdMesh`, `placeFlat` |
| Warm key light, cool fill, long shadows | One shadow-casting `DirectionalLight` (2048 shadow map) and a `HemisphereLight`; in a big world the light follows the area being filmed | `DeskWorld.tsx`, `BoardWorld.tsx` |
| Glow | Unlit materials with tone mapping off, plus additive glow sprites. No bloom pass is installed | `Glow` |
| Haze | `fog` matched to a painted backdrop | `BoardWorld.tsx` |
| Textured surfaces | Wood, plaster, circuit board, keyboard, paper and signs drawn in code on canvases | `toon.ts` |
| Constant camera motion | A shot list of camera keys (position, target, field of view), eased between keys; hard cuts between shots | `WhatIsGPU.tsx` |
| Whip | 9 frames of fast camera travel with a CSS blur on the canvas | `WhatIsGPU.tsx` |
| Impact shake | A decaying offset on the camera for 12 frames | `KICKS` |
| Word-by-word captions | One or two words per caption, pop-in over 4 frames, moved up where a face sits in the caption band | `WordCaptions.tsx` |
| Music and effects | Made in code with numpy, timed to the word timings | `studio/scripts/make_bed.py` |

Lessons from the first video:

- **Frame for portrait.** A wide subject (a screen, a grid) only fits a 9:16 frame from far away. Arrange things deep rather than wide, or film them from above.
- **Keep faces out of the caption band** (about 66 to 74 % down the frame). Check every shot where a character stands in the lower half.
- **Not used yet:** bloom, depth of field, motion blur, rain, time-of-day changes inside a shot, glTF models. Test each with a few stills before relying on it; they all add render time.
