# Video workflow: from topic to finished file

How a video is made from a Claude session with this folder connected. First used end to end for "What is a GPU?" (general theme) on 16 November 2026. The steps are the same for every theme; the theme only supplies the values marked **theme** below.

## The steps

0. **Theme.** Ask the owner which theme this video is for (the list is in `themes/README.md`), then read `themes/<theme-id>.md`. Do this before anything else. Everything below takes its language, cast, narrator and fonts from that file.
1. **Topic and script.** The owner gives a topic and, if they want, a length. Claude writes the script in the **theme**'s language and tone, in the structure from `CLAUDE.md`, at the theme's speaking pace (general theme: about 160 words a minute, so 60 words for 22 seconds), and checks every fact, plus anything the theme's script rules add.
2. **Voice.** ElevenLabs connector, `creative_generate_speech`, with the **theme**'s narrator and model (general theme: Brian, `eleven_v4`). If the theme has no narrator chosen yet, settle that with the owner first. **Generate one take only** (`generations_count: 1`; the owner decided on 17 November 2026, as a second take doubles the cost). Make another take only if the owner asks for one or the first has a clear fault, such as a mispronounced name. About 325 credits for 60 English words; about 12 credits per Hindi word (the 250-word Khar-Dushan script cost 1,491 per take).
3. **Word timings.** Pin the take with `creative_add_flow_asset_node`, then `creative_transcribe_audio` with that node in `connect_from` (about 165 credits for two minutes of audio). The result lists every word's start and end time; write them into the video's `words.ts`.
3b. **Speed (if the theme sets one).** Epic Mythology: 1.15x with ffmpeg `atempo` after download, and word timings divided by 1.15 (details in `themes/indian-mythology.md`).
4. **Audio file.** Download the take's `full_res_url` (from `creative_get_generations`) with curl into `studio/public/audio/<video>-voice.mp3`. The download address `elevenlabsusercontent.com` is allowed for Claude's workspace.
5. **Shot plan.** One row per sentence: place, light, camera move, characters and poses. Follow `reference/toon-3d-style.md` and the **theme**'s cast and world notes.
5b. **Reusable parts added with the first mythology video:** `shared/camera.tsx` (shot lists, eased camera keys, kicks, `Rig`, `useFontsLoaded`, `ramp`), `shared/nature.ts` (sand, earth, grass, rock, bark, foliage, palm fronds, ripples, thatch, brick, sky gradients, flames, clouds), `shared/fx.tsx` (`Glow`, `SkyDome`, `Flame`, `Particles`, `DustBurst`), and in `flat.tsx` `mythicSprite`, plus `scale`, `roll`, `opacity` and `airborne` on `Flat` (airborne figures face the camera from below too, so they never turn edge-on).
5c. **Added with the Jatayu video (17 November 2026):** `shared/epic.ts` for figures wider than the standard one (`drawRavana` with ten heads, `drawJatayu`, `drawJatayuLying`, `drawDonkey`), each with an `*_ASPECT` you pass to `Flat` as `aspect`. A figure lying down gets its own drawing; rolling the standing square drawing made it float. Keep a `story.ts` with functions that say where every figure is at frame f, so the world and the camera read the same positions.
5d. **Added with the Khar-Dushan video (17 November 2026):** big crowds that march, close in and fall (`story.ts` `SOLDIERS`, `soldierAt`; one instanced mesh per look for standing and one for fallen figures, `drawFallen`), glowing arrows timed to each fall (`Arrows` in `khar-dushan/JanasthanWorld.tsx`), a second world in the same video (`world: 'camp'` in the shot list), and a remembered shot (the opening image again with a sepia filter). Lessons: far hills are spheres, so keep the camera well inside 200 m of the origin or it ends up inside one and the sky turns black; a group marching in from far away should narrow into a wedge, or it thins out to nothing; an over-the-shoulder camera must stay within about 12° of the figure or the figure drops out of a 9:16 frame; when the camera tracks a moving figure, move both linearly over the same frames.
5e. **Added with "You are glowing right now" (Mind Matter, 17 November 2026):** `shared/people.ts` dresses the standard figure in new outfits without new code (`drawPerson`: hoodie, lab coat, bare-chested with shorts, scrubs, shirt, checked shirt; hair, bun, beanie, straw hat, cap; glasses, stethoscope; carries a camera case, tablet, clipboard or glow stick; poses stand, sit, point, hold, carry, lookUp, armsUp, shrug, reach), plus `drawRadical` (a spiky free-radical spark), `drawMouse` (side-on lab mouse, `MOUSE_ASPECT`) and `boardTexture` (cached sign boards with lines of text). The head and face are now `drawHead` in `toon.ts`, shared by every outfit. `shared/kit.tsx` has `Box`, `Cyl`, `Ball`, `Board` and `KeyLight` (a shadow light that follows the filmed area) to keep world files short. Music bed for that video: `scripts/make_glow_bed.py`. Lessons: in a 9:16 frame a row of five seated figures only fits when filmed along the row, not from the front; a glow sprite near the camera washes a face out, so dim it in close-ups; a door shutting out all light leaves a black frame, so keep a lit face or sign in the shot.
5f. **Added with the Wojtek video (Undercover, 19 November 2026):** `shared/bear.ts` (`drawBear`: a brown bear in stand, walk, carry with a crate, sit, watch, drink, munch and a cub with a bottle; `BEAR_ASPECT`), and carries `crate`, `bottle`, `cigs`, `tins` and `papers` in `people.ts`. Three separate worlds in one video (dock, camp, Cassino) as `world` values in the shot list, each built at the origin. Lessons: four-sided cones read as pyramids, so army tents are ridge tents (a triangular prism); a flat figure tilts back towards a camera above it, so give seated figures `maxTilt={0.2}` when the camera looks down at a desk; under an awning the camera can't rise far, so widen the fov for top-down inserts; a tracking shot keyed to two points misses a figure that walks back and forth, so use a fixed wide shot of the path instead. A name the voice might mispronounce (Wojtek) is written phonetically in the voice prompt ("Voytek") and spelled correctly in `words.ts`.
6. **Build.** Make a new folder `studio/src/<topic>/`, using `studio/src/gpu/` as the pattern for the shot list, worlds and `words.ts`. Import the reusable parts from `studio/src/shared/` (`toon.ts`, `flat.tsx`, `WordCaptions.tsx`) instead of copying them, pass the theme to the captions (`<WordCaptions words={words} theme={THEMES['<theme-id>']} />`), build new worlds, characters and shots, and register the composition in `studio/src/Root.tsx`. Anything a later video in the same theme could reuse (a costume, a prop, a pose) goes into `studio/src/shared/`.
7. **Stills.** Fastest way: a `<Video>Stills` composition whose frame i renders the video at `STILL_FRAMES[i]` (pass the frame as an `at` prop; Remotion's `<Freeze>` did not work for this), rendered with `--sequence --image-format=jpeg --concurrency=2`: 33 stills in about 15 seconds in Claude's workspace. Then tile them into one contact sheet with ffmpeg. In a vertical frame the visible width is small (about 1.1 x the distance at 46° fov), so keep the camera 5 m or more from a group. Render a handful of single frames (one per shot) and fix framing before any full render.
8. **Render, then mix.** The owner renders the picture without sound on the Mac (Claude gives the command and never renders it itself); then Claude mixes the voice and the music bed with ffmpeg (see below).
9. **Deliver.** Save the mp4 where the **theme** says (general theme: `videos/`) and send it to the owner. **Always share the finished video with a title and a description** (the owner asked for this on 17 November 2026): in the theme's language, with the source named for mythology, a short line in English and hashtags. Save them beside the video as `<video>.md`.
10. **Posting** to YouTube, Instagram and Facebook is done by the owner.

## Rendering

**The owner renders every video on the Mac. Claude never renders a full video, or any part of one, in its own workspace** (the owner's firm rule, 19 November 2026, after Claude started a workspace render without asking). Claude's job ends at stills: it renders single stills and the contact sheet to check framing, then hands over the render command and waits for the owner.

**On the owner's Mac.** Node.js and the studio packages were installed on 16 November 2026. The 86-second Hanuman video (2,582 frames) rendered in under 10 minutes; the 114-second Jatayu video in about 15. In Terminal: `cd ~/Documents/"Content Studio"/studio`, then `npx remotion render <CompositionId> out/<name>-picture.mp4 --props='{"audio":false}'`. Computer use can only click in Terminal, not type, so Claude gives the owner the command to paste and checks `studio/out/` for the result. Mix afterwards in Claude's device shell (`ffmpeg` is installed there), straight from the folder; copying a ~90 MB video back from the cloud workspace timed out. The device shell needs up to 17 GB of free disk on the Mac to start.

**Stills in Claude's workspace** (stills only, never the video): copy `studio/` without `node_modules`, run `npm ci`, and render the `<Video>Stills` composition with `--gl=swangle --browser-executable=/usr/bin/google-chrome`. Never use or reinstall the Mac's `studio/node_modules` from Claude's Linux shell.

**Sound mix** (the balance the owner approved):
`ffmpeg -i picture.mp4 -i voice.mp3 -i bed.wav -filter_complex "[1:a]aresample=48000,aformat=channel_layouts=stereo,apad[v];[2:a]volume=0.3[b];[v][b]amix=inputs=2:duration=longest:normalize=0,loudnorm=I=-14:TP=-1.5:LRA=11[a]" -map 0:v -map "[a]" -c:v copy -c:a aac -b:a 192k -shortest out.mp4`
Make the bed with `studio/scripts/make_bed.py`, changing its effect times to the new video's words.

## Keeping time and tokens down

- **One video per chat.** Start a new chat for each video; everything needed is in this folder.
- **Reuse, don't rebuild:** import from `studio/src/shared/` and follow the pattern of `studio/src/gpu/`. Read only the chosen theme's file, not the others.
- **Look at few pictures.** Each image Claude views is expensive: one still per shot, and one contact sheet of the final video.
- **Never list `studio/node_modules`.** Exclude it from any folder listing.
- **The owner renders on the Mac.** Claude never renders the video itself.
